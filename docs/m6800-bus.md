# MC68000 synchronous M6800 bus: E, VPA, VMA

## 1. Purpose

Besides the asynchronous bus (AS, UDS/LDS, DTACK) the MC68000 has a synchronous interface for M6800-family
peripherals, which have no DTACK and time their transfers on the enable clock E. This document describes the
three signals E, VPA and VMA and the synchronous cycle in rm68000. The same cycle serves the autovectored
interrupt acknowledge (`interrupt-acknowledge.md`). The asynchronous cycle is in `bus-interface.md`.

## 3. Behaviour

From UM 3.7 and Appendix B:

- E: one period is ten processor clocks, six low and four high (60/40 duty cycle). It is produced by an internal
  ring counter that may come up in any state, and it runs whatever the bus is doing.
- VPA (input): the addressed device or area is an M6800 device and the transfer must be synchronized with E;
  during an interrupt acknowledge it requests an autovector. VPA is decoded from the address and qualified by AS.
- VMA (output, active low on the MC68000): the address is valid and the processor is synchronized with E; it
  responds only to VPA.
- The cycle (Figure B-1 on p. B-1, text on pp. B-2 to B-4): the processor starts a normal read or write cycle;
  when VPA is recognized on the falling edge of S4 it inserts wait states until E is low, then asserts VMA; the
  peripheral transfers during the high part of E; the processor latches read data in S6, and negates AS and the
  data strobes half a clock later, in S7, when E goes low; VMA is then negated. The device must remove VPA within
  one clock after AS is negated.
- Synchronization (p. B-4): with VPA asserted early, only the wait states needed to reach E are inserted. Best
  case: VPA recognized on the falling edge three clocks before E rises (three clocks after it falls); worst case:
  two clocks before E rises (four after it falls), when the cycle waits for the next E pulse.
- "Data transfer acknowledge (DTACK) must not be asserted while VPA is asserted ... If both signals are asserted,
  the operation of the state machine is unpredictable" (p. B-4).
- Interrupt acknowledge with VPA: an M6800 read cycle with VMA asserted, vector number generated internally,
  vectors 25-31 (B.2).

## 4. Implementation

### 4.1 E

`e_cnt` counts 0 to 9 on `cen2` and `e = (e_cnt >= 6)` (`e_cnt`, `e`, `m68k_cpu_si.sv`): E is low for six clocks
(0-5) and high for four (6-9). It counts on every `cen2` whether or not a cycle is running. It is reset to 0
(reset branch of `e_cnt`), so after reset E starts low with a fixed phase, a case of the arbitrary power-up state
of the manual. `e_cnt` also goes to the bus FSM, which synchronizes VPA cycles on it.

### 4.2 The VPA cycle

The bus FSM handles VPA in its S4/S5 loop. In the S5 branch, which runs on the falling edge at the end of S4,
`e_cnt` still holds the value before its own update on that edge (note on `e_cnt` above `term_now` in
`m68k_bus_fsm.sv`).

- Recognition: in branch S5 with neither DTACK nor BERR (`term_now`, `m68k_bus_fsm.sv`) and VPA asserted,
  the FSM sets `vpa_wait` and goes back to S4 (branch S5 of `m68k_bus_fsm.sv`).
- VMA: asserted on the S5 edge at which `e_cnt` goes from 3 to 4, provided VPA was seen on an earlier S5
  (`vpa_wait`) or is seen on this one (`vma_go`, `m68k_bus_fsm.sv`; applied to `vma_n` at every phase tick). E rises
  two clocks later.
- End: the FSM leaves the loop on the S5 edge at which `e_cnt` goes from 8 to 9 with VMA asserted
  (the `e_cnt == 8` test of branch S5). From branch S6, on the next rising edge, `rdata` follows `data_in`; branch S7, on the next
  falling edge, is the edge at which `e_cnt` goes from 9 to 0 and E falls: the read data is taken there, and AS,
  the strobes and VMA are negated (branch S7).

The result: VMA goes low two clocks before E rises, stays low through the four clocks of E high, and rises with AS
on the falling edge of E. This timing was measured on the reference core (VPA termination note above `term_now` in
`m68k_bus_fsm.sv`).

Difference from the manual's worst case: a VPA first seen on the S5 edge at which `e_cnt` goes from 3 to 4, four
clocks after E falls, still catches the coming E pulse in the core, because `vma_go` accepts VPA seen on that same
edge (`vma_go`). Appendix B gives that alignment as the worst case, which waits for the next
pulse. The core follows the reference core here; it has not been compared with a physical 68000. A VPA seen on a
later edge waits for the next E period in the core as well.

### 4.3 Priority, BERR, VMA outside VPA cycles

- The S5 branch tests `term_now` before VPA: if DTACK (or BERR) is asserted together with VPA, the cycle ends as
  an asynchronous cycle (branch S5 of `m68k_bus_fsm.sv`).
- VMA is asserted only in the S4/S5 loop of a cycle with VPA, and it is negated in S7 and at reset
  (reset branch and branch S7 of `m68k_bus_fsm.sv`).

### 4.4 Single-phase mode

With `cen` and `cen2` high on every clock (`single_cen`, `m68k_bus_fsm.sv`) S4 and S5 alternate on every
clock and `e_cnt` advances on every clock, so the conditions on `e_cnt` could always fall on S4. The FSM therefore
accepts them in S4 too (`vma_go` and branch S4 of `m68k_bus_fsm.sv`). In this mode, used by the instruction benches, the
VPA cycle completes correctly, but its length against E is not the 68000 one.

### 4.5 Through `m68k_cpu_dt_si`

The generator inside `m68k_cpu_dt_si` asserts DTACK on every cycle with AS and at least one strobe low, unless
`bus_cs` and `bus_busy` are both high or VPA is low (`dtack_assert`, `m68k_cen_dtack.sv`; its `VPAn` input is the
wrapper's `vpa_n`), so a VPA cycle through the wrapper runs synchronous to E with no DTACK (`clock-dtack.md`,
section 4.4; checked by `sim/tb_m68k_cpu_dt.cpp`).
